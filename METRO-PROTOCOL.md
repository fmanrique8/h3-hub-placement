# Madison road-network follow-up

The study uses inhabited 2020 Census blocks whose internal points fall inside the rectangle 43.00–43.16° N and 89.54–89.28° W in Dane County, Wisconsin. Population is a demand proxy. Whole block populations stay attached to their internal points. The candidate set starts as a five-by-five grid inset from this rectangle. Snap grid anchors to the largest connected road component, discard anchors more than 0.9 km from a node, and deduplicate snapped sites. Open three hubs. Candidate names are grid anchor IDs. These are illustrative nodes.

Roads use Dane County 2024 TIGER/Line topology from `TNIDF` and `TNIDT`. Use public motor-road classes S1100, S1200, S1400, S1630 and S1640 with modeled speeds of 90, 60, 35, 40 and 25 km/h, respectively. Treat edges as bidirectional. Charge all demand and representatives the same access speed of 20 km/h for their straight-line connectors to the nearest node in the connected graph. The study uses modeled road times. It omits direction restrictions, turn delays, observed traffic, truck access and routing across the county boundary. Distances along edges use their polylines in a local equirectangular frame around Madison.

Run raw points, fixed H3, spread-based adaptive H3, and weighted k-means. Volume refinement is a secondary comparison. Compare two exact matched budgets given by the occupied logical H3 parents at resolutions 6 and 7. The fixed and adaptive methods roll up a common resolution-8 index. Adaptive starts at resolution 5. Weighted k-means uses the same positive demand weights and the matched bucket count. Evaluate all plans against the same original block-to-candidate time matrix. Report mean objective, weighted p95, membership changes, and relative solver gaps. Point-level raw cost is the best feasible reference. If its gap is positive, use its dual bound to check the cost threshold conservatively.

Use the existing 20-second MILP limit, one solver thread, seed 0, and `1e-6` gap tolerance. Measure three rotated repetitions of the same workload and use medians. Each repetition constructs one common shortest-path table from all candidate nodes and looks up times for each selected representation. Report road ingestion, candidate routing, and methods separately. The routing-inclusive comparison adds the common candidate routing time to each method's time. Download and graph creation are common one-time preparation and excluded from this comparison.

As an independent control, enumerate all raw candidate triples once. Report its timing separately from the common-solver experiment. This checks whether the small candidate set gives a cheaper practical route to an exact plan.

**Continue if** spread-based H3 meets all three targets at an exact matched budget: at least twice as fast as raw after including common routing, within 1% of the raw reference cost, and within 5% of the raw reference p95. Against both simpler comparators it must improve point-level cost by at least 0.5 percentage points or reduce routing-inclusive runtime by at least 10% while staying within the same tolerances of that comparator. A comparison with an unusable incumbent is inconclusive for its budget. Stop after the two matched-budget runs and document the limits of the single geographic case.

**Reporting after the run.** The raw MILP returned different feasible incumbents with uninformative bounds. Keep all three runs and report its best feasible cost, its maximum gap, and its median time. Use independent exhaustive enumeration as the certified objective and p95 reference. Apply the original thresholds twice, using raw MILP and direct enumeration respectively as the timing comparator. The thresholds remain the same in both evaluations.
